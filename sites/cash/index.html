    Illustrative figures.

    01 · Found

    Counted against the books.

    Tills file their closing counts on the daily sheet. Reconciliation runs them against the core system's Teller Cash Report, and every variance, missing entry, over-limit position and late entry becomes an exception, visible on Pulse.

    02 · Explained

    The branch says what it was.

    The branch picks a category and explains it, citing the vault movements already recorded in the app. A movement in the record is better evidence than a figure retyped off a voucher. The item waits for review.

    03 · Reviewed

    Someone else closes it.

    Nobody can close their own explanation. The server refuses it. A reviewer accepts and closes, or sends it back with a reason, and every step is named and timestamped in the audit trail.

    04 · Read

    Forty exceptions, read as what they were.

    Exceptions by Category shows a month as it usually is: mostly transfers and keying, with real cash differences in plain view. Open items age in working days, escalate, and are emailed to the reviewing team at the second level.

    Reconciliation

    Three figures. One owner for every break.

    Pulsus Cash compares teller cash three ways: what the drawer counted, what the teller ledger says it holds, and what the branch control account holds. That is what lets a break be named, not only found.
